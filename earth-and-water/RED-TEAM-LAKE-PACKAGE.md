# RED TEAM — the lake package, attacked by its friends first
*12 August 2026. Every objection we could raise against our own bills, with the current
answer or the honest absence of one. Hand this to the clinic WITH the bills. Nothing
disarms a reviewer faster than arriving with your own objections list.*

*Drafted by a layman with machine assistance. Published in order to be corrected.*

## AGAINST THE PACKAGE AS A WHOLE

**"Three bills is three targets."**
Answer: they interlock now. SPURS v2 charges only metered users, so agriculture must have
a path — Dusty Spurs is that path, and it is load-bearing, not companion. BOOTS's 150%
generation surplus is the same overbuild the storage economics need. Pitch order: Dusty
Spurs first (money already appropriated, farms gain), SPURS as what makes it fair, BOOTS
as what makes it fund itself. One sentence: *we already set the money aside, here is how
it pays for itself, and nobody who can't measure their water gets charged for it.*

**"The drafter is not a lawyer."**
Answer: correct, stated on every page, and it is the reason for the clinic ask. The ask,
verbatim from the paper: bring the bill, or bring a correction to it. Both are useful.

## AGAINST SPURS v2 (consumptive use charge)

**Dormant Commerce Clause.** v1 died here — destination-based trigger, *Sporhase v.
Nebraska* (1982). v2 charges metered depletion evenhandedly and never asks where anything
went; subsection (4) forecloses the reading explicitly. Residual risk: a plaintiff argues
the metered/estimated line is a proxy for out-of-state interests. Answer: the line tracks
administrability (measured vs modeled), falls on in-basin industry and municipalities
alike, and exempts the classic export crop (unmetered irrigation) — the opposite of
protectionist targeting.

**Takings / impairment of water rights.** The charge attaches to use, not title;
subsection (7) preserves every right, priority and seniority. It is a rate, not a
curtailment. Residual: a user argues an unavoidable charge on exercising a right is a
de facto impairment. Answer: users already pay supplier rates and power costs to exercise
rights; a consumption charge at the published commercial rate is of the same kind.

**"The metered/estimated line invites gaming — nobody will meter."**
Real. A user could avoid the charge by declining to meter returns. Mitigations for
counsel: (a) statute already lets the engineer set metering standards under (8)(a);
(b) pair with existing secondary-metering mandates — Utah has been requiring secondary
water metering statewide; (c) the de minimis threshold keeps residential out, so the
avoidance population is small and visible.

**Placement.** Is 73-3 (Appropriation) the right chapter for a charge, or should it live
in 73-10g or a new chapter? Open question for counsel; 73-3-33 is verified vacant.

## AGAINST BOOTS (thermal load facilities)

**The chapter collision.** Title 54 Ch. 25 is occupied; first vacant is 54-28. And Ch. 26
("Large-Scale Electric Service Requirements," active PSC rulemaking, Docket 25-R318-01)
overlaps §203's agreement regime. See BOOTS-AMENDMENTS-v2.md Change 0 for the three
reconciliation options. The overlap is also the pitch: extension of live statute.

**Federal preemption.** The NSA Utah Data Center at Bluffdale sits in the basin and is
exactly a thermal load facility. A state siting statute reaching a federal installation
loses. Fix: express carve-out for federal facilities, so it never becomes the argument.

**Retroactivity.** The 2035 compliance date reaches existing facilities (Meta Eagle
Mountain et al.), who will fight hardest and have local employment numbers. Position:
this is a deliberate negotiating counter, to be traded on purpose — not pre-conceded.

**The 5 MW threshold sweeps too wide.** Heat rejection at 5 MW catches cold storage,
food distribution, some manufacturing. Hospitals and schools are exempt; the grocery
warehouse is not, and it will show up confused and hostile. Options: raise the floor,
add an exemption class for facilities whose primary business is not computing, or
scope to facilities above a compute-power fraction.

**"36 hours of storage is a ban wearing a siting requirement."**
Only if priced in lithium (~3,600 MWh at 100 MW — larger than the largest battery on
earth). Priced as pumped storage, duration is reservoir volume and volume is cheap;
Utah has the relief and the workings. Change 1's safe-harbor list exists precisely so
no analyst scores it in cells. Keep 36; arm it with the list and the cost note.

**"Your own statute bans your reference design."** An open impoundment evaporates;
§201(1) prohibits consumptive rejection. Answered by Change 3: the pool claims §201(4)
(imported fill) with the new clarifying subsection, and the explainer says the number out
loud — open water in Utah loses roughly 3–4 feet a year off the surface, metered by water
balance, against a cooling tower that spends water every hot afternoon forever.

**Rocky Mountain Power.** §203 grants dispatch rights but removes PSC term-setting;
utilities structurally dislike large self-suppliers. Softener available in Change 0
option (c): a self-supplied facility does not cost-shift, which is the entire rationale
for utility protection.

**Economic development whiplash.** Utah courts data centers with tax exemptions; this
bill conditions them. Answer: the below-grade section (Change 2) converts the ask — the
facility that complies GIVES the town its surface. A bill that only takes has nobody in
the room arguing for it; 204 is who argues for it.

## AGAINST DUSTY SPURS (transition program)

Weakest attack surface of the three — it spends already-appropriated money ($152M sitting
unobligated in 73-10g) and names no crop. Residual objections:

**"Paying farmers to fallow is a slippery slope to buy-and-dry."** Answer: the design is
anti-buy-and-dry — conservation capital currently retires farms at $609–25,000/AF; this
frees water at $85–176/AF with the farm still standing and principal returning at 3%.

**"The lease is forever but the money is once."** The instream lease runs annually;
counsel should confirm the funding mechanism for out-years and whether the Watershed
Enhancement Program can hold multi-year lease obligations.

**"Transition failure risk lands on the farmer."** True and worth saying plainly: the
loan is the farmer's, and a failed berry operation is a defaulted loan plus a converted
field. Mitigation: the tier structure grants the capital the state benefits from and
lends only the business half; cold-chain and processing infrastructure (the cooler, the
butcher) is the real de-risker and belongs in the appropriation.

## THE HISTORY THAT WILL BE QUOTED AT US

**The 1973 soybean embargo.** Export controls spooked Japan into funding Brazilian soy;
Brazil is now the largest exporter. Anyone proposing export-side agricultural policy gets
this thrown at them. Our answer: nothing in this package restricts export — v2 charges
non-return of water, identically, wherever the product goes. The embargo history is an
argument FOR depletion-based drafting, not against this package.

**Kelo / eminent domain.** Nothing in the package takes property. The below-grade section
is a condition of new siting, accepted by choice. Any future acquisition interest is
handled the honest way: dedication and easement at entitlement, right of first refusal by
contract — written down on the way in.
