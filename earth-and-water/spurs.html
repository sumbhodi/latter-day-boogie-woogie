What this is

A working draft of a Utah bill, second version. A metered user pays the commercial rate for water withdrawn from the Great Salt Lake basin and not returned to it — measured, not estimated, and without regard to where anything goes. Water that comes back is free. Water that is never measured is out of the bill entirely.

⚠ This is not a tax and not a tariff. The rate is set by §1(d) to the volume-weighted average that public suppliers already charge commercial and industrial customers here. It is the removal of a discount — charged only on the water the basin verifiably never gets back.

⚠ This was not drafted by a lawyer and it is not legal advice. It was drafted by a layman with machine assistance, against the live Utah Code — 73-3-33 verified vacant, every cross-reference verified live, 12 Aug 2026. It is published in order to be corrected.

What changed from v1 — and why

Version one charged water because of where it went. This version charges water because it did not come back. Same lake, same finding, different trigger — and the difference is the whole Commerce Clause.

⚠ Sporhase v. Nebraska (1982) holds water is an article of commerce; a state rule that turns on crossing a state line is the paradigm dormant-Commerce-Clause defect. v1's export trigger was the plaintiff's opening brief. v2 never asks the destination — §4 forecloses the question explicitly — and charges non-return evenhandedly, which is a conservation measure states may make. The finding was already in v1: water consumed within the basin returns in part; water exported returns not at all. The harm was never the destination. It was the non-return. v2 charges exactly that.

It still never names alfalfa

§4 applies the charge "without regard to the destination, market, or purchaser of any product… the product, crop, animal, beverage, container or process involved." The bill does not know what alfalfa is — and it no longer knows what a state line is either.

No farmer pays a dollar

The charge falls only on metered users — withdrawal and return measured by device. Use determined by crop coefficient or evapotranspiration model is expressly out (§1(c), §6(a)). An estimate is a fine basis for a grant and a poor basis for a tax: every coefficient becomes litigation, every dry year an exception.

⭐ Agriculture participates through the companion bill instead — the Agricultural Water Transition Amendments, where the coefficients calculate what a farmer is paid, not what they owe. The v1 fight over who writes the alfalfa coefficient is deleted with the coefficient. Metered-versus-estimated is an administrability line, not a geographic one — and the parties it reaches already hold meters, already retain counsel, and already understand rate proceedings.

Your rights survive intact

§7 is the load-bearing section and a hostile reader should be sent to it first. Nothing in the section limits, conditions, curtails, forfeits or reduces a water right; requires a change application; prohibits the production, sale, export or import of any crop, animal or product; or authorises the state engineer to deny an application by reason of it.

⭐ The right is untouched. The return is credited. The non-return is priced.

The hard part, named

The metered/estimated line invites a dodge: don't meter. A user could avoid the charge by declining to measure returns. §8(a) hands the state engineer metering and verification standards, Utah's secondary-metering mandates already close most of the gap, and the de minimis threshold keeps households out — so the avoidance population is small and visible. But this is where the drafting attention belongs now, and it is an honest trade: v1's unwinnable constitutional fight for v2's winnable administrative one.

⭐ An impoundment's loss is a water balance, not an argument — gauge, inflow, outflow, rainfall; solve for evaporation. §5(c) writes that method into the bill. The facilities this reaches can be charged honestly because they can be measured honestly.

The questions for counsel

Four, none of them Sporhase. Whether the metered/estimated line lives better in statute (as drafted) or in the engineer's rules. Whether imported water gets a full exemption (as drafted, matching the thermal-load bill) or a credit. Whether the section needs express severability. And whether "the waters of the basin" is the right return standard, or return to a named tributary should be required.

The instrument

BASIN CONSUMPTIVE USE AMENDMENTS

LONG TITLE

General Description:
        This bill establishes a charge on the consumptive use of water of the Great Salt Lake
basin by metered users, measured as water withdrawn and not returned to the basin.

Highlighted Provisions:
        This bill:
        ▸ charges a metered user for water withdrawn within the basin and not returned to it;
        ▸ applies the charge identically without regard to the destination, ownership, or
intended market of any product;
        ▸ applies only to users whose withdrawal and return are measured, and expressly does
not apply to use determined by estimate or coefficient;
        ▸ provides that this bill does not limit, condition, curtail or forfeit a water right;
        ▸ requires the state engineer to publish the rate annually; and
        ▸ deposits collections in the Great Salt Lake Watershed Enhancement Program.

Money Appropriated in this Bill:
        None

Other Special Clauses:
        None

Utah Code Sections Affected:
ENACTS:
        73-3-33, Utah Code Annotated 1953

Be it enacted by the Legislature of the state of Utah:

Section 1. Section 73-3-33 is enacted to read:

73-3-33. Charge for consumptive use of basin water.

        (1) As used in this section:
                (a) "Basin" means the Great Salt Lake watershed as defined in Section
65A-16-101.
                (b) "Consumptive use" means the volume of water withdrawn from within the basin
by a metered user, less the volume returned by that user to the waters of the basin, determined
under Subsection (5).
                (c) "Metered user" means a person whose withdrawal and return are measured by
device, including a person served by a public water supplier, and does not include a person
whose use is determined by crop coefficient, evapotranspiration model, or other estimate.
                (d) "Rate" means the volume-weighted average rate charged during the preceding
calendar year by public water suppliers in the basin to commercial and industrial customers.

        (2) The Legislature finds that:
                (a) water withdrawn within the basin and returned to it remains available to the
basin;
                (b) water withdrawn within the basin and not returned to it is not available to
the basin, without regard to where it goes or what is made from it; and
                (c) a charge that does not distinguish between the two charges the basin for the
difference.

        (3) A metered user shall pay the rate for that user's consumptive use.

        (4) The charge under Subsection (3) applies without regard to:
                (a) the destination, market, or purchaser of any product;
                (b) whether a product is sold, used or consumed inside or outside the basin;
                (c) the product, crop, animal, beverage, container or process involved;
                (d) the residence, citizenship or place of organization of the user; or
                (e) the identity, priority date or seniority of any water right.

        (5) Consumptive use is determined by measurement. A user shall report, and the state
engineer may verify:
                (a) metered withdrawal;
                (b) metered return to the waters of the basin; and
                (c) for an impoundment, evaporative loss determined by water balance from
gauged stage, metered inflow and outflow, and recorded precipitation.

        (6) This section does not apply to:
                (a) a person whose use is not measured, including a person whose use is
determined by estimate or coefficient;
                (b) water conveyed into the basin from outside it, or the use of that water;
                (c) water used for human consumption, sanitation, or food service;
                (d) fire suppression, including the charging and testing of a system; or
                (e) a use during a state of emergency declared under Title 53, Chapter 2a.

        (7) Nothing in this section:
                (a) limits, conditions, curtails, forfeits or reduces a water right;
                (b) requires a change application under Section 73-3-3;
                (c) prohibits the production, sale, export or import of any crop, animal or
product; or
                (d) authorizes the state engineer to deny an application by reason of this
section.

        (8) The state engineer shall make rules under Title 63G, Chapter 3, Utah Administrative
Rulemaking Act, establishing:
                (a) metering, reporting and verification standards;
                (b) the water balance method for an impoundment; and
                (c) a de minimis threshold below which this section does not apply.

        (9) The state engineer shall publish the rate under Subsection (1)(d) on or before
January 31 of each year.

        (10) Money collected under this section shall be deposited in the Great Salt Lake
Watershed Enhancement Program created in Section 65A-16-201.

        (11) This section takes effect for consumptive use occurring on or after January 1,
2030.

Section 2. Effective date.

        This bill takes effect on May 4, 2028.
