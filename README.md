# Latter Day Boogie Woogie

**A one-stop shop for saving the Great Salt Lake: the art and the legislation, in the same place.**

Four drafted bills, a costed business plan, and a gallery. Two of the bills pass on their own.

---

## Start with the one that cannot lose

**POLISHED BOOTS** says a new data centre in Utah must run closed-loop, must not touch potable
water, must not consumptively use water from within the basin, must generate 150% of its own peak
demand renewably, and must hold 36 hours of storage.

It appropriates nothing. It has one opponent. And it has no bad branch:

| | |
|---|---|
| **no data centre comes** | the lake loses nothing. The law sits there costing $0 |
| **a data centre comes** | it arrives closed-loop, off-basin, over-generating, and paying into the grid |

**It is a ratchet, not a bet.** Every other proposal in this space asks someone to spend money now
against a benefit later. This one asks nobody for anything.

**→ [Read POLISHED BOOTS](earth-and-water/boots.html)** · 1,675 words · severable · no cross-references

## Steal this HOA bylaw

Nothing in BOOTS is new. **Arizona has required a 100-year assured water supply before you can build
a subdivision since 1980. California has required solar on new homes since 2020.** BOOTS says do
both, for data centres. It is a parking minimum. It is a covenant. It is the most boring instrument
in this repository and that is the entire point.

**Which means it is not about Utah.** Every state with a data-centre boom and a water table has this
problem, and the structure ports without modification — the closed loop, the non-potable rule, the
own-generation requirement, the storage floor. Only the basin definition is local.

⭐ **Take it. Rename it. Put your own state in the caption. The legislative text is CC0 — public
domain, no attribution required, no permission needed.** That is what it is for.

*The local flair is **[SPURS](earth-and-water/spurs.html)**, which prices water leaving a terminal basin in any
wrapper — including the water embodied in a crop. That one is Utah's fight, and it is a decade long.*

## Nobody agrees on the number, including the state

```
    4,212 ft    the art          ≈ the 1986–87 record high (4,211.6)
    4,205 ft    this bill        set from litigation exposure
    4,200 ft    Utah Rivers Council's "4,200 Project"
    4,198 ft    ⭐ the Great Salt Lake Strike Team — the STATE'S OWN healthy minimum
    4,188.5 ft  November 2022 record low
```

**This bill targets seven feet above the number Utah published about its own lake.** That is a
choice, it is arguable, and the argument belongs in public. The deficit at 4,198 is **800,000
acre-feet per year** (Strike Team); this plan is sized at 1,000,000.

## What this still owes, before anyone acts on it

Published incomplete on purpose, because the fastest way to fix a bill is to let people who know
better read it.

- ⚠ **BOOTS's placement in Title 54 is a judgement call and is unverified.** The `§54-17-601`
  cross-reference is unchecked. The 2035 date and the peak-load definition are underived.
- ⚠ **SPURS has not had counsel review against *Sporhase v. Nebraska*** (dormant Commerce Clause).
  The §5(b) broker provision is an open decision, not an oversight.
- ⚠ **The long bill's §408(4) delivery rate reaches target by 31 Dec 2075. Nobody derived that date.**
- ⚠⚠ **And the big one: buying the agricultural water outright is cheaper than building the corridor
  at every price we modelled.** That is disclosed in the plan's risk register rather than buried —
  §10, open item O15. The counter-argument is there too. Judge it yourself.

**If you know Title 54, or Utah water law, or what it actually costs to cool a rack — corrections
are the most useful thing you can send.**
